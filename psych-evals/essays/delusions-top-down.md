# Delusions May Occur Without Aberrant Salience

## Objective

Discuss the hypothesis: 
    *In individuals with symptoms of MDD and DID, delusional thinking occurs via rigid top-down inference instead of psychotic states associated with schizophrenia.* 

## Overview
1. Introduction & Typical Delusions in Psychotic Disorders
1. Atypical Delusions in MDD & DID
1. Comparative analysis of how/why delusion occurs in psychotic individuals vs. MDD/DID individuals
1. Limitations
1. Conclusion & Relevance


## Notes:

- A delusion is the brain's irrational attempt to make sense of normal underlying signals. The individual with atypical delusions demonstrates a learned behavior of irrational reasoning and applies this excessively to normal signals. Motivation for learned behavior is desire to avoid effects of traumatic event. 
- Delusion in this case is a cognitive issue more than a neurobiological issue.
- What starts off as disbelief via cognitive rigidity turns into a delusion, with no dopaminergic symptoms. During this development, social connectivity and basic human needs become gamified as tools for control, replacing normal survival drives with a rigid, top-down cognitive strategy.
