## Notes:
- Individuals with these three mental illnesses are remarkably resistant to positive symptoms that would make them candidates for schizophrenia diagnosis. 
- The common framework for interpreting prediction error in individuals with mental illness is this: Rather than being a failure of basic intelligence, a delusion is often the brain's rational attempt to make sense of distorted underlying signals. However, this is what occurs exclusively in schizophrenic individuals, where dopamine dysfunction alters perception and salience. The neurobiological basis for schizophrenia is increased dopamine firing and increased dopamine reception. The target individual of this discussion is an individual with MDD with psychotic features and DID. These two individual types have no similar biological etiology. Therefore, this framework cannot apply.
- The proposed framework for interpreting predictive error in this case individual is this: A delusion *is* a failure of basic intlligence. It represents the brain's *irrational* attempt to make sense of *normal* underlying signals. This occurs because an individual demonstrates a learned behavior of irrational reasoning and applies this excessively to normal signals. Motivation for learned behavior is unclear. 
- This lack of clarity arises from the fact that survival instinct, even extreme cases of poor reasoning (i.e.: mental disability), overrides any learned behavior. This is especially true when the individual must rank the value of a mental behavior and a physical result of said behavior. Humans are able to withstand severe mental states for longer than they are able to withstand severe physical states. Learned irrational reasoning should at least be temporarily unavailable during an extreme or sustained conflict. Death is a greater threat than insanity. 
- Another giveaway: mood issues present in this case individual are aggressive but not external, and being internal result in the impairment of the individual and rapid decline of quality of life.
