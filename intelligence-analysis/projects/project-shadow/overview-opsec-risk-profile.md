### Background
American adversaries use data on the social and behavioral patterns to weaken the psychology of Americans, on an individual and societal level. Individuals in law enforcement, politics, and public service are key targets of this type of attack. This attack is a form of terrorism. Data that can be used to analyze and mainpulate the psychological state of an individual is data we should protect, for the individual American psyche informs the ethos of our society. The danger of this type of psychological attack is the difficulty for individuals to effiiently resolve the side effects of this offense through religion, social connection, medicine, or psychotherapy.

In solution to this problem, a personal affairs OPSEC service may be anonymously offered by individuals, specifically in the target professions mentioned above. These said individuals (the analysts), although anonmymous, must be willing to study their subjects and apply techniques in data science and psychology to build a personal affairs OPSEC risk profile. Upon the completion of this profile, the analyst may suggest a course of action for the target individual. This defense would involve subtle changes to the target individual's order of personal affairs. Offense in this regard would entail strategic psychological posturing and thought-projection, with the aim of disguising said target's identity, hence making the collection of adversary intelligence linked to this psychological warfare difficult to obtain.

*Note: We are presently faced with a plethora of offensive tactics administered by our top global adversaries. New technologies are manufactured to attack the vulnerabilities of the most important members of our society. Current events, such as the current developing case of the several missing scientists and diplomats affected by anomalous health incidents (i.e.: Havana syndrome) point to the potential existence of this threat.
